# Retail Media Monetization Benchmark, India — Executive Summary

**A benchmark of retail-media ad monetization across three listed Indian marketplaces, built
entirely from public disclosures — and a finding about which of them actually disclose the number
that matters.**

## Headline result

**Of Swiggy, Nykaa, and Eternal, only Swiggy discloses advertising revenue as a clean, standalone %
of GOV.** Nykaa and Eternal both disclose a number that's easy to mistake for it — their own
advertising *spend* — which this project deliberately excluded rather than conflating with ad
*revenue*.

| Company / segment | Period | GOV/GMV | Ad revenue disclosure | Implied ad revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swiggy — Food Delivery | Q4 FY26 | INR 9,005cr | ≥4% (disclosed floor) | ≥INR 360.2cr |
| Swiggy — Instamart | Q4 FY26 | INR 7,881cr | 6-7% (forward target, not current actual) | INR 512.3cr at midpoint |
| Nykaa (FSN E-Commerce) | FY26 | INR 19,963cr | INSUFFICIENT DISCLOSURE | — |
| Eternal (Zomato/Blinkit) | — | not sourced | INSUFFICIENT DISCLOSURE | — |

## Resume-ready line

*"Benchmarked retail-media ad monetization across three listed Indian marketplaces from public
disclosures; found only 1 of 3 discloses ad revenue as a standalone % of GOV (Swiggy, ≥4% in food
delivery), and correctly excluded two companies' ad-spend figures that are commonly mistaken for ad
revenue — deriving an implied INR 360-512cr quarterly ad-revenue range from the one clean
disclosure."*

## Why this finding matters more than a forced rate card

A rate-card benchmark built by treating "advertising expense" as "advertising revenue" for Nykaa and
Eternal would have produced a clean-looking three-company table — and been wrong. This version is
less tidy and more defensible: it shows exactly what's known, what isn't, and why.
